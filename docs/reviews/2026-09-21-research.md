# Research evidence for Kiln's primitives and authoring direction

Research checked 2026-09-21. This is a targeted primary-source review, not an exhaustive systematic review or a reproduction of published experiments. Dates below are first public paper submissions unless otherwise stated. Results belong to each paper's own benchmark and implementation. Architectural recommendations are explicitly our inference, not experimentally established conclusions about Kiln.

## Decision supported by the evidence

Keep executable source, deterministic geometry, named parts, and iterative inspection as the foundation. Improve the contracts between operations and parts before expanding the catalog. Treat semantic labels such as vehicle or architecture as optional discovery and evaluation metadata; express actual output requirements separately. Preserve specialist recipes when they demonstrably help. No reviewed paper establishes that one mandatory asset taxonomy, or the removal of all taxonomies, improves generation universally.

Kiln already exposes a compact overview, operation search, exact helper signatures, and runtime capability discovery through `kiln_list_primitives` (`docs/tools.md:9-75`). Therefore progressive discovery is an existing product feature to evaluate and improve, not a missing architecture requiring a second tool system.

## Evidence ledger

### 1. ProcFunc: closest match to the library design question

**Published:** 2026-04-29. **Evidence:** [paper, sections 3 and 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.26943v1), [author repository](https://github.com/princeton-vl/procfunc).

The authors make Blender operations explicit typed functions, isolate implicit state, standardize generator inputs/outputs, and distinguish deterministic generators from explicitly seeded random samplers. Their from-scratch material experiment reports coding-error reductions from 30.3% to 4.9% for Gemini 2.5 Pro and 44.7% to 21.4% for GPT-5.2. This supports investing in interface quality even when models are capable. The comparison uses a nonstandard harness, documentation and examples; it does not isolate naming from all other changes. Parameter-editing results are mixed: aggregate material editing errors rise from 1.3% to 2.1%, while geometry errors are nearly unchanged. The paper does not establish that every unified abstraction improves every task.

**Kiln inference:** prioritize explicit coordinate frames, consistent returns, validated parameter meaning, and substitutable generator interfaces. Test unification per use case. Avoid citing the strongest from-scratch result as a blanket improvement in geometry generation.

### 2. 3DCodeBench: execution success is insufficient

**Published:** 2026-05-31. **Evidence:** [paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.01057v1), [author project](https://www.3dcodebench.com/).

The benchmark evaluates twelve VLMs on procedural modeling and combines executable-output checks, geometric/perceptual metrics, and human pairwise preferences. It reports API mismatches as a major failure source, and disconnected or floating geometry even among rendered outputs. Iterative refinement improves performance. Its perception/preference correlations are measured across model results; they do not establish per-asset structural correctness. Arena comparisons and several quality metrics condition on successful renders, making their denominator different from overall request success. The curated procedural factories also constrain the domain represented by the benchmark.

**Kiln inference:** report compile success, structural validity, visual acceptance, and downstream usability separately. Inspect all failures, not just successful gallery outputs. Use paired human comparisons to evaluate helper changes, but retain deterministic structural checks and import/playback checks. More thinking tokens alone are not a demonstrated substitute for repairing faulty contracts.

### 3. Proc3D: compact representations help, but constrain output

**Published:** 2026-01-18. **Evidence:** [paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.12234v1).

Proc3D develops a compact procedural representation supporting parametric editing. Its authors explicitly identify dataset limitations and cube-only generation; their out-of-distribution tigers and airplanes remain cube-based. A method can generalize semantically while retaining a severe geometric vocabulary bias.

**Kiln inference:** retain an escape route to custom geometry. A convenient preset must not become an implicit shape ceiling. Evaluate curves, thin shells, openings, organic profiles, and nonorthogonal structure, rather than accepting object recognition alone as success. This is evidence against restricting Kiln to a small shape vocabulary, not evidence that its current helpers should be discarded.

### 4. PartCrafter: compositional outputs have real utility

**Published:** 2025-06-05. **Evidence:** [paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.05573v1).

PartCrafter uses part-specific latent tokens and hierarchical attention to jointly generate decomposable meshes, including inferred hidden parts. Its authors identify a relatively small training corpus of 50,000 part-level examples as a limitation. A generated part decomposition is not the same as a dimensionally interchangeable component interface, manufacturable assembly, or proven rig.

**Kiln inference:** preserve semantic part identity and transforms throughout editing and export. Measure whether a part can actually be replaced, moved, or animated without breaking neighbors. Do not claim “composable” merely because the GLB contains multiple meshes.

### 5. KaiNinja: very recent evidence favors preserving part boundaries

**Published:** 2026-09-14; revised 2026-09-15. **Evidence:** [primary paper abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15659v2).

This new preprint extends TRELLIS.2 with a dual-volume representation to preserve touching-part interfaces that its single-volume surface representation cannot express. Its training includes agent-authored parts. Authors report improved whole-object and part metrics. These are very recent author-reported results; this review has not reproduced them or audited its dataset, released code, or benchmark fairness.

**Kiln inference:** the strategic value of source-authored parts is not limited to making attractive renders. Assembly boundaries and part provenance may also serve downstream editing and data generation. This is a reason to protect that structure, not a reason to launch a training-data product or add this model before packaging.

### 6. TRELLIS.2: generic 3D does not imply closed solids

**Published:** 2025-12-16. **Evidence:** [Native and Compact Structured Latents for 3D Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.14692).

The proposed O-Voxel representation targets open, non-manifold, and enclosed surfaces with material attributes. Its objective and output representation differ from a CAD solid system. The paper's broad quality claims concern its evaluated generation setting and do not provide a universal production-readiness guarantee.

**Kiln inference:** distinguish renderable surfaces, closed solids, and fabrication-ready geometry. A blanket watertightness requirement would wrongly reject legitimate cloth, leaves, panels, and architectural surfaces. A permissive surface policy would be inadequate for printable solids. These are explicit requirements or validation profiles, independent of whether an asset is called a prop.

### 7. CAD-Recode: code is useful, but CAD is a separate contract

**Published:** 2024-12-18; revised 2025-03-11. **Evidence:** [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.14042), [author project](https://cad-recode.github.io/).

CAD-Recode reconstructs sketch/extrude programs from point clouds as executable Python and trains on one million procedural sequences. Its experiments concern CAD reconstruction and editable code. They do not establish arbitrary organic mesh generation or general environment authoring.

**Kiln inference:** adopt useful ideas such as profiles, exact parameter meaning, and construction history where practical. Do not relabel a GLB mesh workflow as CAD, or add a CAD kernel solely because research uses one. Precision modeling, general surface modeling, and scene arrangement can share concepts without sharing all topology requirements.

### 8. Infinigen Indoors: environments require relations

**Published:** 2024-06-17. **Evidence:** [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11824), [CVPR paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/papers/Raistrick_Infinigen_Indoors_Photorealistic_Indoor_Scenes_using_Procedural_Generation_CVPR_2024_paper.pdf).

Infinigen Indoors combines procedural assets with a constraint language and an arrangement solver. Spatial relations, accessibility, placement, and contextual rules participate in scene composition. This is an indoor synthetic-data system; its solver and asset library are not a turnkey solution to every environment or monument.

**Kiln inference:** a coliseum is not adequately modeled by increasing a building's floor-count limit. It needs repeated bays, tiered seating, circulation, openings, coherent dimensions, hierarchy, and appropriate views. Treat a single exportable environment asset and a scene of referenced assets as related workflows with explicit output boundaries. Introduce relations only where actual tasks justify them; do not preemptively port an entire constraint solver.

### 9. Scene Language: semantics need not dictate topology

**Published:** 2024-10-22. **Evidence:** [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.16770).

Scene Language separates programmatic hierarchy/relations, natural-language semantic classes, and visual identity embeddings, supporting different rendering approaches. This supports a distinction between what an entity means and how it is represented. It is not an evaluation of Kiln's API, and does not prove that categories should disappear.

**Kiln inference:** keep semantic labels usable for search and recipes while specifying geometry and behavior through capabilities. A stadium's roof, vehicle hull, and lamp shade can all benefit from shell/profile operations even when their semantic labels differ.

### 10. ESG: dynamic scenes introduce another independent dimension

**Published:** 2026-09-14. **Evidence:** [primary abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15392).

This recent preprint combines a machine-checkable scene graph, spatial optimization, physical attributes, and event timelines for engine-executable dynamic scenes. The reported study covers only ten scenes. Its claims should not be generalized to all simulation tasks.

**Kiln inference:** articulation, collision, physical behavior, and temporal events are separate capability axes. Calling an object a vehicle does not establish any of them. They need distinct acceptance criteria if supported. Dynamic simulation is not an immediate prerequisite for packaging a static asset authoring engine.

### 11. Progressive tool discovery and tool design

**Published:** 2025-11-24 and 2025-09-11 respectively. **Evidence:** Anthropic's [advanced tool use](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/advanced-tool-use) and [writing effective tools](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/writing-tools-for-agents). These are first-party engineering reports, not independent academic replications.

The discovery report describes lower context consumption and improved internal MCP evaluation accuracy when loading relevant tools on demand. It also acknowledges search latency and weaker benefit for small, compact, frequently used libraries. That evidence concerns large tool catalogs, not whether searching six geometry helpers is superior to directly including their signatures. The tool-design article recommends evaluation-driven iteration on tool boundaries, descriptions, responses, and examples.

**Kiln inference:** preserve a small stable MCP surface and improve the helper catalog behind it. Measure query-to-relevant-helper recall, unsupported assumptions, and time to first valid asset. Offer operation synonyms and prerequisite/limitation summaries. Do not introduce separate MCP tools for every primitive or add another agent framework merely to obtain discovery.

## Proposed architecture hypothesis to test

This section is design inference, not a claim that research has validated it for Kiln.

Use four layers:

1. **Geometry operations:** shapes, profiles, sweeps, lofts, surfaces, transforms, patterns, topology operations, and custom mesh escape routes. Each has a precise contract.
2. **Composable parts:** reusable generators with documented local axes, origin, dimensions, material slots, attachment frames, and return structure. Different geometry implementations can satisfy the same attachment contract.
3. **Optional recipes:** wheels, stairs, walls, seating tiers, roofs, hulls, and other named assemblies. Names remain valuable search cues; recipes should not restrict which operations can be composed.
4. **Asset requirements:** bounds, scale, resource budget, surface/solid intent, material/export needs, articulation, scene scope, and review views. Semantic tags provide context without silently selecting all these policies.

“Agnostic” should mean reusable contracts across domains. It should not mean a single function with dozens of mutually dependent modes, erasing useful semantics, or claiming every part fits every socket. Interchangeability is relative to an explicit interface: a lamp shade and vehicle fairing may share a shell constructor, but not necessarily an attachment frame or acceptance criteria.

## Adversarial experiments before committing to migration

Run the same briefs under current categories, a neutral entry point with capability discovery, and explicit specialist recipes. Keep model, effort, render budget, and acceptance process comparable. Use several runs rather than one success. These model runs require explicit spend authorization; this research did not execute them.

Include familiar and unfamiliar briefs: a small prop, articulated mechanism, car or boat, multi-storey structure, coliseum, thin organic surface, and an environment assembled from repeated components. Include edits, not just first drafts: replace one part, resize the parent, change bay count, change material, change export scale, and preserve articulation. Reserve some combinations until after helper design to reduce overfitting to demonstrations.

Measure request completion with failures included; time/tokens/tool calls; discovery recall; actual helper usage; custom-geometry usage; invalid parameter recovery; attachment and clearance defects; normal/UV defects where relevant; parameter-edit locality; export/import survival; visual acceptance; and worst-case geometry/resource growth. Keep taste judgments separate from deterministic validity.

Reject the hypothesis if neutral discovery causes repeated omissions, more custom-code errors, or worse output despite similar effort. Retain specialist recipes where they earn their place. Reject unification if its interface becomes harder to select, explain, or validate than two focused operations. A newer model is a new test subject, not evidence that old contracts are either obsolete or correct.

## Packaging implications

The evidence supports a narrow immediate investment: reliable signatures and examples, known-limit disclosure, helper and part conformance checks, exact editability, good feedback, and honest output claims. It does not justify a pre-release rewrite around a new DSL, neural generator, constraint solver, or mandatory category system. Whether the legacy local agent should be maintained or retired requires repository and usage evidence, not these papers. Its age alone is insufficient.

No engine implementation, public deployment, package publication, live model run, or neural-model installation was performed for this research.
